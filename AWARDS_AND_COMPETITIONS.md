# Awards & Competitions Guide

> Programs change eligibility rules, age limits, categories and deadlines every year. **Always read the current official rules** before applying. Descriptions below are general orientation, not guarantees of fit.

## Before you submit anything
1. Replace all simulated metrics with real ones (or label them clearly as prototype data).
2. Add real validation: user interviews, a pilot conversation, or a domain-expert review.
3. Prepare: 1-page summary, 2–3 min demo video, live demo link, repo link, pitch deck (see PITCH_DECK.md).

## Programs to research
| Program | Orientation | Angle for this project |
|---|---|---|
| **MIT Solve** | Global challenges; solutions with real-world traction | Needs evidence of users/pilots; frame around a specific challenge (e.g., climate resilience) |
| **Conrad Challenge** | Student innovation (check age/team rules) | Environment & sustainability category; emphasize technical build and business plan |
| **Diamond Challenge** | Student entrepreneurship (check age/team rules) | Lead with business model and market validation |

| **WSA Young Innovators** | Digital-for-good content and apps (check current format) | Position as digital solution for sustainability |
| **Global Youth challenges / National founder competitions** | Vary widely by country and year | Search current listings in your country; adapt the same core materials |
| **UNESCO innovation / youth programs** | Education, science and sustainability initiatives | Emphasize the Learning Hub and open data aspects |

## Strengthening a submission
- **Be specific:** one city or region, one user, one problem — then show how the platform generalizes.
- **Show evidence:** even 5–10 interviews with teachers, municipal staff or NGO workers beat generic claims.
- **Be honest about AI:** "rule-based now, ML models planned with backtesting" is credible; "94% accuracy" without data is not.
- **Show impact metrics you'll measure** (see IMPACT_REPORT.md).
- **Team & roles:** document who did what.

## Suggested timeline
Month 1: validation interviews + real data for one region → Month 2: replace mock data for 2–3 modules, record demo → Month 3: polish materials, apply to the programs whose rules you fit.
